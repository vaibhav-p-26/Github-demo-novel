Jay Gatsby - a mysterious and ambitious man who appears to have everything—wealth, parties, and charm—but is driven by a deep longing for a past love, Daisy Buchanan. He built his fortune through relentless effort and self-invention, hoping to recreate the life he imagined with Daisy. Beneath his glamorous exterior, Gatsby is vulnerable, lonely, and haunted by the idea that love and status can erase the past.

Nick Carraway - a thoughtful and observant young man from the Midwest who moves to New York to learn the bond business. As Daisy’s cousin and Gatsby’s neighbor, he becomes the quiet witness to their complicated lives. Nick is honest, introspective, and morally conflicted, often caught between fascination with the rich and disgust at their emptiness. His perspective gives the story its sense of realism and emotional depth.

Daisy Buchanan - graceful, charming, and beautiful, but also shallow, indecisive, and deeply influenced by wealth and social appearance. She is both the object of Gatsby’s dreams and a symbol of the unattainable life he longs for. Daisy’s voice and presence are intoxicating, yet her choices reveal a lack of courage and a preference for comfort over genuine love. She represents the seductive power of wealth and the emptiness beneath it.

Thomas “Tom” Buchanan - a wealthy, arrogant, and physically imposing man whose confidence is matched only by his cruelty. He comes from old money and believes in his own superiority, treating others with dismissive contempt. Tom is domineering, possessive, and deeply hypocritical, especially in his treatment of Daisy and his own affairs. He embodies the moral decay and social entitlement that define the world Gatsby is trying to enter.
